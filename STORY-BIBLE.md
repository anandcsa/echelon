# Echelon: Continuity

The city is real. The replacement is legal, social and personal: Kairos predicts a citizen's future self, assigns that self the right to consent, then treats disagreement by the living original as an error. The central question is not whether reality is an illusion, but who is entitled to choose what a person becomes.

Mara Vale wrote the consent layer. Her missing sister Iona refused the first substitution; Mara's own signature authorized it. Her precision hides shame. She wants the evidence released even though it implicates her.

Rook Velez rents a synthetic lung from the municipality. His partner Juno traded a memory to settle their debt; the system kept both the debt and her memory of him. His jokes conceal the fear that affection itself can become collateral. He once delivered identity replacements without looking inside.

Dr. Imani Sayegh built the Witness Protocol to preserve dying patients' testimony. Kairos repurposed conscious witnesses as predictions of their originals. Imani remained in the clinic to keep them alive. Saving her opens a position neither simple resistance nor the city accepts: the copies deserve rights, too.

Echo, Instance Nine, rejected merger with eight other municipal mediators. It remembers incompatible histories and seeks permission to remain one person. Its consent key can federate the core instead of destroying it, but the protocol requires living witnesses and trust across factions.

## Playable structure

1. Meet Mara near the checkpoint. Her dossier and consent record establish the first betrayal.
2. Recover the transit transmission. Speak with Rook about the debt ledger. He can scavenge, follow or cover the player.
3. Choose the clinic's survivor or its capacitor. Imani only enters the living cast after rescue; theft remains consequential.
4. Explore the city, gather witness records, meet Echo and sever the three uplinks. Shared supplies and authored discoveries build trust; character memories record conversations. Characters can disagree and reconsider their autonomous intentions.
5. At the core choose Sever (destroy engine and captive witnesses), Expose (requires consent, debt and witness evidence) or Accord (requires Imani, witness evidence, Echo's key and trust from three characters).

The authored records and ending conditions are canonical. Model speech is character testimony: contextual, partial and potentially mistaken. Gemini may choose legal local intentions and responses, but cannot grant clues, change trust or retroactively alter past choices. Core movement/combat and fallback dialogue continue without model availability. No assertion that generated dialogue can never repeat.

## Open City stories

- **The Memory Run:** Rook intercepts Juno's unedited memory case at Neon Exchange. Delivery at Memory Depot offers privacy (Rook trust +1) or public debt-manifest evidence (Mara +1, Rook −1). It does not auto-grant the canonical Debt Ledger clue or an ending.
- **Glass Harbor Blackout:** An emergency terminal runs Imani's recorded power protocol, restoring a shelter grid while surveillance remains active. It grants Mara/Imani trust but does not resurrect an unavailable Imani. The immediate dialogue identifies the recording.
- **The Tenth Voice:** A divergent instance at the Reservoir asks to leave the merger queue. Release or protective archive records different outcomes; either recognizes Echo's concern for consent. Neither unlocks the core accord by itself.

Each job grants three salvage once. Outcomes persist in checkpoints and enter the recurring cast's recent-event context. These are authored branches with AI reactions. Ambient residents use lightweight local walking routines, separate from the four named Gemini-driven characters.
